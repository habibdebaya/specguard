specguard

Formal-logic verification of aerospace safety standards

Aerospace safety standards govern how civil aircraft systems are developed and certified. Boeing, Airbus, and COMAC certify their aircraft against ARP4754B, the SAE recommended practice. The document is 172 pages long, written in English and tables, reviewed by humans, and not formally verified.

specguard encodes its requirements as Z3 constraints and runs three families of generic logical checks. Applied to ARP4754B Appendix E, the standard's own worked example of a wheel brake system, it surfaces two real defects. Both emerge from the algebra of the encoded constraints, with no heuristic targeting either finding.

The gap

A document of 172 pages, structured into many overlapping tables, restated and refined across multiple revisions, can contain logical defects that survive review. Two requirements may use the same identifier but state different obligations. A safety claim stated in one section may be restated in another in a different unit, with the rewrite failing to preserve the original claim's strength. A propositional condition with two directions may have only one direction propagated to the contract that downstream suppliers build against.

Each individual statement reads sensibly. The inconsistency only emerges when statements are paired up.

specguard automates that pairing.

The approach

Each numbered requirement is encoded as a Req(id, text, constraint) entry, with the verbatim English preserved alongside a Z3 expression that captures its logical content. Boolean variables represent propositions; real-valued variables represent probabilities, rates, durations, and quantities. Symbols are reused across requirements that name the same physical concept. Conditional language becomes Implies, conjunction becomes And, probabilistic bounds become inequalities.

The full Appendix E was encoded across three system layers (airplane, WBS, BSCU), 141 numbered requirements in seven named collections. Where the standard restates the same claim in different units, the conversion is made explicit through axioms derived from section E.3.3, which establishes that flights average five hours.

Three check families

  • Internal consistency. Each collection is jointly satisfiable.
  • Directional entailment. Every constraint in the target follows from the entailer under the axioms.
  • Bidirectional equivalence. Two requirements declared as restatements entail each other under the axioms.

Failures produce witness states extracted from Z3's model.

Finding 1: an asymmetric encoding of a paired conditional

Table E13 of Appendix E, the proposed safety requirements from the WBS PSSA, contains two paired entries:

E13-6. When "HYD 1 Enable" output is enabled, then "Alt/Emer Ctrl" output shall be disabled.

E13-7. When "HYD 1 Enable" output is disabled, then "Alt/Emer Ctrl" output shall be enabled.

Together these specify a biconditional. At any moment exactly one of the two control signals is active, so a working braking path is always available.

In Table E19, the WBS Requirements Specification, only one of the two directions is propagated. Requirement S18-WBS-R-6109 captures E13-6 verbatim. There is no corresponding requirement for E13-7's converse direction.

The directional entailment check on (SPEC_REQUIREMENTS, PSSA_REQUIREMENTS) reports E13-7 as failing. The witness state Z3 produces:

alt_emer_ctrl_on = False
hyd1_enable_on   = False

This is a state where neither control signal is active. The PSSA forbids it; the SPEC permits it. A supplier building strictly to the SPEC carries no formal obligation to rule it out, even though the safety analysis identifies it as a state where braking is unavailable.

Finding 2: a unit mismatch between Tables E14 and E28

Table E14 lists seven safety assumptions from the WBS PSSA. Two of them concern probabilities of input-loss events:

E14-4. The probability of loss of an airplane electrical power bus will be less than 1.0E-04 per flight.

Table E28 is the master assumption catalogue for the development. It restates the seven WBS PSSA entries with a slight rewording. For five of them the restatement preserves the unit basis. For entries 4 and 5 it does not:

E28 WBS PSSA ASMP 4. The failure rate for loss of an airplane electrical power bus will be less than 1.0E-04 per hour of flight.

The numeric value is identical (1.0E-04). The unit basis has changed from per-flight probability to per-hour failure rate. Under the five-hour flight from section E.3.3, a per-hour bound of 1.0E-04 corresponds to a per-flight probability of approximately 5.0E-04, five times the per-flight bound Table E14 sets.

The two formulations are encoded with distinct symbols, related through the axiom block. The bidirectional equivalence check reports:

E14-4  ⇔  E28-WBS-ASMP-4         NOT EQUIVALENT

E14-4 entails E28-WBS-ASMP-4     PASS
E28-WBS-ASMP-4 entails E14-4     FAIL

witness: p_loss_elec_bus_per_flight = 4.8828e-04

The witness value sits below 5.0e-04 (per-hour bound satisfied) and above 1.0e-04 (per-flight bound violated). The two restatements are not equivalent. The same shape repeats for E14-5 and E28 ASMP 5.

The asymmetry in the rewrite is the signature of a transcription event. Five entries were carried through unchanged; two had their units flipped without an accompanying numeric adjustment. A unit-preserving restatement would have produced two formulations Z3 could prove equivalent. The actual restatement does not.

Closing

Both findings have a common structure. They survived human review because each individual statement reads sensibly. The defect requires pairing requirements, applying a conversion or a symmetry, and noticing that the pair fails to compose. Reviewers reading sequentially do not perform this composition; algebra does.

The methodology generalises. Aerospace standards beyond ARP4754B use the same layered PSSA/SPEC structure, the same per-flight and per-hour conventions, the same restatement of upstream claims into downstream specifications. The encoding shape is reusable. The check families take no domain knowledge. specguard runs whatever the requirements file declares.
